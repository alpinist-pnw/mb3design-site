## The Pipeline

**Model → Fabrication → Install**

Every piece planned before it leaves the shop. Every building connection resolved before fabrication begins.

---

## Phase 1: As-Built Investigation

For work in existing buildings, the first step is understanding what is actually there — not what drawings say. 3D laser scanning captures every ceiling joist, rafter, column, out-of-plumb wall, window placement, and floor anomaly as a high-resolution point cloud.

The exhibit is designed to fit the building as it exists, not as it was documented.

---

## Phase 2: Digital Design and Virtual Prototyping

The complete exhibit or installation is modeled in 3D at fabrication-grade resolution. This is not a presentation model — it is the model from which all drawings, CNC files, and BOMs are derived.

- Organic surfaces: Rhino 3D
- Structural engineering: SolidWorks
- Parametric variation and iteration happen in the model. Each variation costs hours, not material.

The model is fit-tolerance checked against the as-built scan data. Every connection to the building structure is resolved. Every structural tie-in is validated.

---

## Phase 3: Drawing and Documentation

Shop drawings are derived from the 3D model. Part files are drawn for each unique fabricated component. BOMs are generated. Part numbers are assigned and tracked.

The drawing set is the fabrication contract — what the model says, the shop builds.

---

## Phase 4: Fabrication

CNC g-code comes from the model. Waterjet and laser programs come from the model. Every piece cut from the model has a part number. Every part is traceable.

Multiple fabrication methods may run in parallel on the same project: CNC router, waterjet, laser cutting, brake forming, surface engraving.

---

## Phase 5: Trial Assembly and Verification

Where project complexity warrants it, assemblies are trial-fit in the shop before shipping. Issues caught in the shop cost hours. Issues caught on site cost days.

---

## Phase 6: Installation

Because the building connections were resolved in the model against the as-built scan, installation is execution, not problem-solving. Leveling, alignment, and fit tolerances are designed in — not discovered and patched on site.

---

## The Economics

Installation is 33%+ of total project cost. When it goes wrong, it does not go 10% wrong — it goes catastrophically wrong. The front-loading investment in digital design and virtual prototyping is not overhead. It is risk mitigation for the most expensive phase of the project.
