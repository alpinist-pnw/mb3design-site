## How MB3 Works With Architects and Clients

MB3 positions as the bridge between the architect's design intent and the physical installation. The architect designs the vision — MB3 engineers the buildability.

**The working model:**

The 3D model is the shared artifact. Architect, client, and fabricator all review the same digital object. Iterations happen in the model, not in the shop. When a design change is made, it propagates through the drawings and fabrication files — not through a chain of redlined drawings.

This was the discipline applied on the Paul Allen yacht (architect + shipyard + 12+ trades coordinating around a single 3D model) and carried forward into museum work at Pacific Studio and MB3.

## Handling Impossible Briefs

When an architect of the caliber of Olson Kundig hands MB3 a brief for a flowing organic landscape that thousands of children will climb on inside a historic National Park Service building, the job is not to negotiate the brief down to what's conventionally buildable. The job is to engineer a path to building it as designed.

That requires being able to evaluate fabrication approaches — stack-laminate vs. engineered lattice, for example — and make a recommendation grounded in material science, structural engineering, NPS constraints, shipping weight, and installation risk. Not just design aesthetics.

The recommendation has to be defensible. That defensibility comes from having the full project inside the 3D model before the conversation happens.

---

## Design Development Through Fabrication

MB3 participates in design development, not just fabrication. Parametric modeling allows rapid iteration on form — dozens of surface studies in the time it would take to build a single physical mockup. The client and architect see options that are already proven buildable, because the model that generates the presentation is the same model that generates the shop drawings.

What you see is what gets built.
