## Project Overview

**Company:** Packard Bell / NEC — Ark Interface
**Role:** Director of UI Design and 3D Animation
**Years:** 1996–2001
**Product:** Planet Oasis — a navigable 3D internet city
**Distribution:** Shipped on 3 million computers per year

---

## What It Was

Planet Oasis was a navigable 3D city where every building was a branded destination — sports, finance, entertainment, and other districts. Users could buy real estate in different districts and navigate the environment as a 3D interface to the internet.

It shipped on Packard Bell/NEC machines at 3 million units per year. It was a decade before anyone used the word "Metaverse."

---

## Why It's in the Career Arc

This project sits at the junction of 3D modeling, interactive product design, and mass-market software. It demonstrates that Mark's computational design background predates both the museum fabrication era and the AI era — and that the instinct to use digital tools to build experiential environments has been consistent across every medium.

It also demonstrates the dot-com cycle directly: the project ended with the bust, and Mark returned to building physical things. That return — from virtual environments back to physical craft — is the arc that shaped the cross-domain pattern recognition that drives the current work.

Tools used included Softimage XSI, Mental Ray, and 3DS Max.
