## Project Overview

**Client:** Paul Allen
**Vessel:** M/Y Octopus
**Role:** Project Architect
**Years:** 2000–2003
**Scale:** $250M, 410-foot superyacht

---

## The Work

Full 3D interior coordination for one of the largest private yachts in the world. Rhino was used to model the complete interior — every guest and owner space. The model was shared with the shipyard, key stakeholders, and coordinating trades: audio, IT, interior design.

---

## Why This Project Matters

This project was executed at a time when BIM (Revit 1.0) had just launched in 2000 and was not widely adopted outside traditional architecture — and essentially unused in marine work. Running a fully coordinated, document-controlled common 3D model for a vessel of this complexity was a frontier approach.

The discipline established here became a template: front-load the model, coordinate in virtual space, use the model for client sign-offs and trade coordination, then port to working 2D shop drawings with confidence and reduced rework.

This is the pattern that was later applied — consciously — to the Bay Area Discovery Museum. Different industry, different scale, different constraints. Same structural approach: virtual prototyping as the primary coordination tool.

---

## The Cross-Domain Transfer

The yacht taught: when you coordinate in virtual space before cutting or welding anything, the fabrication and installation phases compress dramatically. When that lesson was brought to museum fabrication — an industry that was still largely working from 2D drawings and resolving conflicts on site — it was a significant competitive advantage.

"I recognized the BADM problem as structurally identical to the yacht problem because I'd lived both. AI sees inside one project. I see across twenty years of them."
