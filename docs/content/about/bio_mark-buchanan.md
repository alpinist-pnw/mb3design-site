Mark Buchanan founded MB3 Design on a single conviction: the right computational tools, directed by deep cross-domain judgment, make previously impossible work not just possible but repeatable. He started building as a carpenter at fifteen, studied architecture (NYIT, then MIT M.Arch), and joined the Rhino pre-beta team around 1994 — knowing Bob McNeel, Rhino's founder, since 1986, and Scott Davidson since the early 1990s. He now runs MB3 Design, building AI agent systems for design and fabrication coordination — and applying the same principle he's always followed: take the best available tools, figure out how they serve the craft, and build something that wasn't possible before.

## Full Biography (press / speaking)

Mark Buchanan is a computational designer, master fabricator, and founder of MB3 Design. For 20+ years he has translated complex architectural visions into built, museum-grade reality — working at the intersection of computational modeling, precision fabrication, and deep material knowledge.

He entered computational design early. A member of the Rhino pre-beta team since approximately 1994, he has a direct relationship with the tool's development going back to Bob McNeel in 1986. He is not a practitioner who adopted computational tools when they became standard — he is someone who helped make them standard.

His career arc is intentionally cross-domain:

- **Architecture school (NYIT → MIT M.Arch)** — computational tools from the outset
- **Packard Bell/NEC (1996–2001)** — Director of UI Design and 3D Animation; led the "Planet Oasis" 3D Internet City product, shipped on 3 million computers per year. A navigable 3D city where every building was a branded destination — a decade before the word "Metaverse" existed.
- **M/Y Octopus (2000–2003)** — Project Architect on Paul Allen's $250M, 410-foot superyacht. Full 3D interior coordination in Rhino shared with shipyard, trades, and vendors — virtual prototyping discipline applied at a scale most architects never attempt.
- **Custom machine manufacturing (2004–2012)** — Brought a manufacturing operation from 2D drafting boards to parametric 3D (Autodesk Inventor), CNC, and purchasing. Learned what it means when the design model becomes the production model.
- **Pacific Studio (2014–2021)** — Director of Technical Design. Led 80+ museum exhibit projects. Scaled the technical design team from 12 to 32 as the company grew from 59 to 132 people. Developed the front-loading methodology that became studio practice.
- **Facture (2022–2023)** — Director of Operations at a product development consultancy spanning electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, and industrial design.
- **Code2Matter (2023–2025)** — Project Lead for Glenn Kaino's 60-foot clipper ship at the Intuit Dome (26 steel pylons, sand-cast aluminum backboard sails) and the 150-foot "Distance of the Sun" origami sculpture at LAX Metro (3D-printed, resin-infused, scaled 10x from real origami, 3D scanned).
- **MB3 Design (now)** — Building AI agent systems for design and fabrication coordination. Testing on a real ADU project in Seattle — real permits, real money — before moving to client work.

His thesis: cross-domain pattern transfer is the judgment layer that makes AI useful in fabrication. AI finds patterns inside a project. Judgment — built from decades across marine, technology, manufacturing, and museum work — recognizes when the problem in front of you is structurally identical to one solved in a completely different industry years ago. That recognition is where real decisions are made.
