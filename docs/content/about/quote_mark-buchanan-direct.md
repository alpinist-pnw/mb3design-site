## The Core Thesis (exact, from source — podcast-prep brief)

> "After 80 museums, a mega yacht, public art, industrial machine design, software — you start seeing that the same problem keeps showing up wearing different clothes. AI is incredible at finding patterns inside a project. But recognizing that the problem in front of you is the same one you solved in a completely different industry ten years ago — that's judgment. And that's where the real decisions get made."

> The shop floor is cheaper than the job site.

## The "Model Caught It" Story — Spoken Version (exact, from source)

> "The tree sculptures tie into the ceiling joists above for seismic stability. But there were only a few joists we could use, and they weren't evenly spaced. The trees are locked into the landscape base — everything's interconnected. If a tree has to shift two inches to find a joist, the entire exhibit moves with it. That's not a fix — that's a catastrophe. Because we scanned every joist and checked the model against as-built conditions, every connection was resolved before we cut a single piece. The install crew walked in and it was where it needed to be. That's what 4,500 hours of front-loading buys you."

## The Career Spoken Intro (~90 seconds — draft from podcast prep, not a direct quote from Mark)

**Note: This is a drafted spoken intro from the podcast prep document, written in Mark's voice, not a recorded or transcribed quote. Use accordingly.**

"I went to architecture school, did two and a half years at MIT's Media Lab, and then the internet happened — I ended up directing a 3D interface product that shipped on three million computers a year at Packard Bell. We built a virtual city where you could buy real estate in different districts — sports, finance, entertainment. This was the late '90s, a decade before anyone said 'Metaverse.' And then the dot-com crash happened, and I went back to building things with my hands. I've been a carpenter since I was fifteen. And that's the thread — I'm a builder. Sometimes it's a $250 million yacht for Paul Allen, sometimes it's a children's museum for the National Park Service, sometimes it's a 60-foot clipper ship sculpture at the Intuit Dome. Right now it's AI agent systems that manage design and fabrication. The medium changes. The instinct is the same: take the best available tools, figure out how they serve the craft, and build something that wasn't possible before."
