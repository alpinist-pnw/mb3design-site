## Computational Design

MB3 Design has been doing computational design since the tool category existed. Mark Buchanan was on the Rhino pre-beta team around 1994 — before the software had a public release — and has been building with parametric and computational tools continuously since.

**What this means in practice:**

Parametric surface modeling in Rhino generates organic, compound-curved forms that cannot be drafted by hand. Hundreds of unique component profiles — each one different — are generated, structurally validated, and output to CNC g-code without manual redraw at each step. Changes propagate through the model.

The model IS the fabrication plan, not a representation of it. When the model is complete, the drawings and CNC files follow from it. This eliminates the gap between design intent and fabrication output that causes rework.

**Tools:** Rhino (primary — since pre-beta ~1994), Grasshopper, SolidWorks (structural engineering and manufacturing), Autodesk Inventor (parametric manufacturing models), ZBrush (organic sculpting), SolidWorks, Revit

**Output:** 3D models, parametric surface studies, shop drawings, CNC g-code, BOM/parts lists, nesting layouts, assembly sequences

**Scale demonstrated:** Single components (waterjet-cut steel plates) to full exhibit environments (three-building museum complex, 564 pages of shop drawings)
